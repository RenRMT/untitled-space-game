# Station idle: the plan

Nov 1, 2026

The name is **Cold Start** (it was Waystation while it was being built, after the first station you build); it still gets checked against store listings before it goes on one. It's a portrait idle game. You bring a dead system's stations back online one at a time, set up the production chain between them and the haulers that link it, and let it run on the real clock while the app is closed. Coming back means collecting what was made, selling it, and spending the money on the next station.

Almost everything it needs is already in `src/rules`. The game itself runs production, haulers, restoration and research on the real-world clock, and settling them comes out the same however long the game was closed. That is exactly the engine an idle game needs. The new work is mainly a source of raw goods, the screens, and the numbers.

## Where it lives

It sits beside the game and the race, built the same way (it uses the game's UI kit, `ui/kit.js`, `ui/ui.js` and `ui/glyphs.js`, which the lint rule allows; nothing else of `src/ui`):

- `idle/index.html` holds the page, `idle/manifest.webmanifest` makes it installable, and `idle/sw.js` is generated by `tools/app-sw.js` (the race's, made to serve both). Its icons go in `assets/idle-icon*`.
- The code goes in `src/idle/`. It imports `src/core`, `src/art`, `src/rules` and `src/world`, and never `src/game` or `src/ui` (the UI kit CSS is fine, as the race uses it). A `no-restricted-imports` block in `eslint.config.js` enforces this, as it does for `src/race`.
- The idle game sets its own numbers and never changes the game's tables. Where a shared module has its table built in, it gets a factory, the way `Lab.create` already serves both the game's research and the race's hangar (see Shared groundwork).
- It keeps its own save (`idle.save`, through `Store` and `Save.debounced`, with `Save.migrate` steps), separate from the game's and the race's.

## The loop

1. **Extract.** Drills on asteroids and moons and scoops on gas giants produce raw goods (iron, silicates, deuterium, then titanium ore, tungsten, rare earths, sulfur) and, at the salvage yard, components.
2. **Refine and make.** Orders at each station run their recipes and draw on that station's own stock.
3. **Haul.** Shuttles and routes carry each station's output to the next one in the chain. A station starved for an input shows the gap and offers a one-tap fix: "Route steel pipe from the ore works".
4. **Sell and deliver.** Goods are sold at a market for credits, or delivered against contracts (below).
5. **Grow.** Credits and goods pay for the next restoration project, more extractors, and research.

A session lasts one to three minutes: collect, sell, queue the next things, and close the app. The long waits (gear from 2 minutes to 2 hours, research, and restorations to come) give a reason to come back.

## Raw goods: extractors

This is the one mechanic that's new. In the game, the player mines raw goods by hand, and no recipe makes them. In the idle game:

- An **extractor** is a recipe with no inputs: `{ in: {}, out: { iron: 1 }, time }`. `Industry.start` already handles one. Nothing is missing, so it only checks for room, and it stops when the station's stock is full. That gives the classic idle "storage full" cap without new code.
- Each extractor sits at a body in the seeded system, which decides what it can pull: asteroid fields give iron and silicates, gas giants deuterium, and moons and planets the surface goods (`Goods.type(kind).surface`). Its process is `extract`, a new bay in the idle game's own recipe table.
- Extractors are bought with credits and then upgraded. Each level adds speed, and the price grows by about ×1.15 per level, the usual idle curve. The table lives in `src/idle/config.js`.
- Salvage: the salvage yard has a **wreck field** extractor that slowly turns out the four component kinds, weighted by `Goods.pick`.

**As built (Phase 2):** each extraction site is a station as far as `Industry` is concerned: the asteroid field, each planet and each moon has its own stock (key `site:<kind><id>`, type `site`, process `extract`). A drill is an order there running `extract.<good>`. A site takes as many orders as it has drills (the env's `slotsAt`), and holds 40 (`roomAt`). Its goods go to the nearest online station that uses what it pulls, or the waystation when none does, by hand until its shuttle is bought (see "Manual, then automated"). So drills, shuttles, the full-storage cap and time away all come out of `Industry.settle` with nothing new. For now a drill is a fixed rate, and more drills are the upgrade; levels can come later.

**As built (wreck field):** the salvage yard gains a second process, `salvage`, beside its recycler: `salvage.<component>` recipes with no inputs (90 s a component), one kind per order, picked by the player rather than weighted by `Goods.pick`, so a run's output stays fixed. They need no research. The recycler's orders then take the components from the same station's stock. Costs and recipes elsewhere keep `Config.SWAP` (components swapped for refined goods), so nothing before the salvage yard asks for one.

## Charting and building (as built)

There's one seeded system per save (`SolarSystem` from a seed), and **nothing is built in it at first**: only the star, the planets, their moons and the asteroid field. The stations are the game's (the waystation, mining camp, minor station, transport hub, salvage yard, major station and capital, by name, culture and art), but the player builds them, in that order (`Config.BUILDS`, run by `Restoration.create` with nothing online).

- **Charting.** At the start only the asteroid field is charted. Every other body is a signal on its orbit (a dashed ring and a `?`); a moon only shows once its planet is charted. A survey charts one body: one at a time, credits × 1.55 per survey done, and a time that grows with the distance to the nearest charted body (minutes). Charting shows the body's art, type and deposits, and about half of them turn up a derelict worth some credits (seeded per body). Drills go on charted bodies only.
- **Building.** A station goes up in orbit round a charted body the player picks (planets and the field have two berths, moons one), and sits where its body is, so hauling distances come from that choice. The waystation is free and comes first; it is the market and the bench, and its stock pays for every station after it. The ore works costs credits only; the rest cost goods at the waystation plus credits. A built station sends what it makes to the waystation (by hand until its shuttle is bought), the ore works starts on steel pipe, and drill sites that were sending to the waystation switch to the new station if it uses what they pull.
- The restoration scene from the plan became the build scene (Phase 4, second pass).

## Selling, and contracts

- **Markets.** `Economy.market` and `Economy.trade` at each online station. Selling a lot in one place drives the price down. The stock recovers halfway every `halfLife`, so spreading sales across stations, or waiting, pays. This adds strategy without any new rules. A per-good **auto-sell** setting (research) turns surplus into credits while the app is away, up to a price floor.
- **Contracts.** These are requests from stations or factions (`world/factions.js` names, crew and android portraits) for *N* of a made item by a deadline, with a bonus on top of market price and sometimes a component reward. Three are open at a time and refresh daily from the seed. They pull the player toward the next tier: "4 deflector Mk II" when you don't have a shipyard yet.

**As built (contracts and auto-sell):** a new set every UTC day, seeded from the system and the day (`IdleRules.contracts`), three (four with the contract desk). Each asks for some of one item that a built station, or the next to build, can make with what's researched or one package on, or a good the drills pull. It pays the item's price × 1.6 (plus 25% per Brokers level), delivered whole from the waystation's stock, and lasts until the day ends. The set depends only on what the player built and drilled and the research done by the day's start, so it comes out the same however time away is settled. Faction names only for now: no portraits or component rewards yet. Contracts sit at the top of the waystation's sheet (the Market tab), which shows a dot when one can be delivered.

Auto-sell (Trade research) is picked per good in the waystation's stock. At every 15-minute step of the clock, the waystation sells the picked goods while their price is at least 75% of usual (50% with Auto-sell II). Production settles step by step to those fixed times, so a week away comes out the same in one settle or many. The away sheet lists what was auto-sold.

## Research

A `Lab.create` instance over the idle game's own packages, with the same shape as `src/race/career.js`:

- **Industry:** order speed, storage racks, order slots, a longer lab queue, more haulers per route, and hauler capacity. These reuse the bonus keys that `Industry.env` already reads (`speed`, `storage`, `bays`).
- **Extraction:** yield per extractor kind, and new extractor kinds (surface goods behind a survey).
- **Blueprints:** gear from Mk II up, like the game's `bp.*`.
- **Trade:** auto-sell, a price floor, a fourth contract slot, and better contract bonuses.

**Trade, as built:** Auto-sell, Auto-sell II, the contract desk (a fourth contract), and Brokers I–II (contracts pay 25% more each), researched at the waystation.

**As built (Phase 3):** the lab is `IdleRules.lab`, a `Lab.create` over `Config.PACKAGES`: the game's own blueprint, mods, industry and logistics packages (`rules/research.js`, the same ids, so `Recipes.known` unlocks their recipes), plus Extraction (`ext.drill.1–3`, +25% drill speed each, through a new optional `rate(recipe, t)` on the Industry env). Squad logic, hull refits and tuning are left out. A package is paid in credits and goods from the waystation's stock, and can be queued once the station type of its category is built (`Research.CATEGORIES` station). Speed, racks, order slots, the lab queue, routes and haulers all apply, each from when it finished (`doneAt`), so a settle split across a finish comes out the same. The idle game has no salvage yet, so every salvaged component in a package's cost or a recipe's inputs is swapped for a refined good (`Config.SWAP`: hull → steel pipe, weapon and computer → carbon fibre, drive → hydrazine); the recycler's recipes keep theirs and wait for the wreck field. Trade research is still to do.

## Offline time

- On load and on `visibilitychange`, settle up to now. Do it in slices of `Industry.MAX_EVENTS`, yielding between slices (`settle` returns `truncated`), so a month away doesn't freeze the page. Show a progress bar if it takes more than a frame.
- Then show a **"While you were away"** sheet: the time away, `Industry.summary`, `Industry.delivered`, auto-sales, research finished, and any orders now starved, each with a fix button.
- There's no offline cap at first: storage limits already stop runaway growth. If balancing needs one, cap the settled time (for example 12 hours, raised by research). Never speed time up beyond the real clock.
- No push notifications: that needs a server. An optional local reminder can come later through the service worker.

## Screens (portrait, one thumb)

- **System:** drawn like the game's own system map, in the Line art style: the star in the middle, the planets and the field on evenly spaced orbits, moons on small orbits round their planets, stations in orbit round their bodies. Everything goes round on the real clock (inner orbits faster). Haulers fly their routes; what was made floats up. Pan and pinch to zoom; tapping a thing follows it and zooms in, keeping it clear of the sheet. Too small for their art, planets and moons are dots in the map's colours.
- **Sheets** in the game's UI kit (`src/ui/kit.js`, `Kit.sheet`, option rows, meters, choices; `kit-line.css`), over the map without darkening it: a body (survey, deposits and drills, stock, shuttle, the stations in orbit and building the next there), a station (stock and selling, orders, shuttle), the survey report, and time away. The kit's icon tab bar along the bottom: System, Survey, Build, Market. A header like a docked station's: the system's name, the next thing to do (tap it to go there), the credits.
- **Chain:** the `Industry.pipelines` view, with each step's stations, flows, gaps and unrouted links. This is the place to fix a broken chain.
- **Lab** and **Contracts** (later).

**As built (Phase 3):** six tabs: System, Survey, Build, Chain, Lab, Market. The Chain sheet lists `IdleRules.fixes` first (a good made at one place and used at another that nothing carries: route it, or before the hub, point a shuttle; an input nothing makes: order it at a station that can, or build one), then each chain's orders and what links them. A station's order shows where each input comes from (`Industry.lineage`) with the fix beside it, and can be paused, switched to another recipe, or put first among the station's orders. The transport hub runs routes of two stops (chosen goods from one place to a station), 2 to start and more with route planning; a route between the same two places as another adds its goods to it.

## Shared groundwork (in `src/rules`, without changing behaviour)

- **`Industry.create({ recipes, process, storage, slots })`**: the module currently reads `Recipes.RECIPES` and `PROCESS` directly in several places (`reserved`, `stop`, `status`, `canMake`, `pipelines`). Make it a factory and keep `Industry` as the game's instance with today's tables, so the game is unchanged and `tests/run.js` keeps proving it. The idle game builds its own instance with the `extract` bay added.
- **`Recipes` lookups for item info** (`Recipes.item`, `value`) stay shared. The idle game's table only adds extractor recipes and re-times the rest.
- **`Restoration.create(list, online)`**: the same treatment, so the idle game supplies its own project list.
- Logistics, Lab, Economy and Goods are usable as they are.

## Manual, then automated

`notes/idle-research.md` is the research behind this. Automation feels like a reward when the player has done the job by hand for a short while, chooses and pays for the automation, and sees it switch on. In an idle game it's also what keeps going while the app is closed. Until this pass, almost everything ran on its own from the start. Now four jobs start by hand, and each is automated when the player buys or researches it. The manual way stays possible after.

| Job | By hand | Automated by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hauling | "Send hauler" on a drill site's or station's sheet: it takes up to 40 (`Config.HAUL`) of what's there and flies it to where it goes (`IdleRules.haulTarget`) | A shuttle bought for the link (`Config.SHUTTLE`: 300 cr × 1.4 a shuttle already running). With Haulage contracts (transport hub research), shuttles are free and new stations and drill sites get theirs. |
| Orders | An order at a station runs a batch, about an hour of work (`Config.FOREMAN.batch`), then waits for "Run again" | A foreman bought per station (credits and goods from the waystation, `Config.FOREMAN.cost`): its orders go on while their inputs last. Drills and the wreck field have no batches. |
| Contracts | "Deliver" | The contract clerk (Trade research). At every step of the clock, it delivers what the waystation can spare once the next station's goods are set aside, and leaves the contracts the player marks. |
| Surveying | Picking each signal | The probe network (Extraction research). At every step, with no survey under way, it starts the quickest one while the credits cover twice its price. It can be switched off. |

**As built:**
- Settling (`IdleRules.settle`) now steps from one moment that matters to the next:
  - a survey's end;
  - a hauler sent by hand arriving (`state.hauls`);
  - while anything runs on its own (auto-sell, the clerk, the probes), every step of the clock (`Config.STEP`, 15 minutes).

  All of these are fixed times, so time away comes out the same however it's settled. The tests check this for each loop.
- A hauler's arrival is a delivery in the log, so the away sheet and the map count it. What doesn't fit where it goes goes back where it came from.
- The away sheet lists what stopped for want of automation: a full place nothing carries from, with "Automate"; a finished batch, with "Hire foreman" or "Run again". The header's next thing to do points at hauling, buying the shuttle, a finished batch and an affordable foreman.
- Hand-sent haulers fly on the map like shuttles.
- Save version 7. Older saves keep their shuttles and get a foreman at every station built, so nothing they had stops.

**Numbers after this pass.** The sim's player:
- hauls by hand from every place with goods and no shuttle;
- buys a link's shuttle at twice its price (the field's first at the price);
- runs finished batches again;
- hires a foreman once it has twice the credits;
- researches the probes, the clerk and Haulage in its order.

Haulage moved to the transport hub's logistics (after Route planning I, 40k cr, 12 h), because at the waystation it came within a day and made every shuttle free. The later foremen cost half the first guess. The manual phase is short, as the research says it should be: 1 to 3 haulers by hand before the first shuttle.

## New systems (prestige)

Once every station is built, the Build sheet offers to leave for a new system. Everything in this one stays behind (stations, drills, research, credits), and the credits earned here (sales, auto-sales, contracts and derelicts, never minus what was spent: `state.earned`) become **legacy points**, floor(√(earned / 10,000)), kept for good (`state.legacy`: jumps, points, and each system left). Each point adds 2% to every order's and drill's speed and 2% to what markets and contracts pay, in every system after (`Config.LEGACY`). The square root means staying keeps paying less and less, so moving on beats sitting on the credits, which is what the open point under Numbers needed.

The next system is rolled from the one left and the jumps made (`IdleRules.nextSeed`), on the current layout, so it's fixed and not re-rollable by reloading. The page writes the new save and loads again on it. Leaving is asked twice (a Jump and a Stay under a warning).

`npm run idle:sim -- --legacy=N` plays a system with N points, and the sim says how many leaving would earn from the shipyard on. Seed 1:

| | No legacy | 30 points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transport hub | day 1 | 14 h |
| Shipyard | day 10 | day 7 |
| First Mk V | day 19 | day 17 |
| Points for leaving at the shipyard / day 30 | 15 / 30 | 15 / 40 |

So leaving at the shipyard earns about 15, and staying the month about 30; a second system with 30 comes about a third sooner, and earns more for staying.

## Phases

1. **Groundwork** (done): the `Industry.create` and `Restoration.create` factories, with tests showing the game is unchanged. Then `idle/` and `src/idle/` with the lint rule, the save, and a bare system view.
2. **The core loop** (done): extractors, the ore works and bench, the station sheet, selling at the waystation, the first two restorations, and the offline settle with its "while you were away" sheet. Built: `idle/index.html`; `src/idle/` (`config.js` for the numbers, `rules.js` for the rules (Node, tested in `tests/run.js`), `save.js`, `map.js` for the system canvas, `sheets.js` for the bottom sheets, and `main.js`). Selling also takes gear and consumables, at 0.8 of their price. Every restoration is playable, but the costs are first guesses until `tools/idle-sim.js` (Phase 4) measures them.
3. **The whole chain:** every station, shuttles and routes, the chain view, contracts, and the lab. Built: routes, the chain view, order controls and the lab (save version 3; version 2 saves gain an empty lab); then contracts, the wreck field, auto-sell and trade research (save version 4).
4. **Balance and polish:** `tools/idle-sim.js`, the restoration scene, haulers on the map, haptics (`core/feedback.js`), the PWA (manifest, icons, service worker), and the name. Built: the sim and a first pass of numbers (above), haptics through `Feedback`, and the PWA (`idle/manifest.webmanifest`, `assets/idle-icon*`, `idle/sw.js` from `tools/app-sw.js`, which now serves the race too). Haulers already fly on the map. Then the second pass: the build scene, the name (Cold Start), systems that differ by seed, and the balance points under Numbers.
5. **New systems** (done): leaving a system with every station built for a new one, with legacy points from what was earned (save version 6; version 5 saves start with none, and count earnings from then).
6. **Manual, then automated** (done): hauling, orders, contracts and surveys start by hand and are automated by shuttles, foremen, the contract clerk and the probe network (save version 7; version 6 saves get a foreman at every station built). `notes/idle-research.md` is the research behind it.
7. **Later:** the extras above, and those at the end of `notes/idle-research.md` (a legacy shop that carries automation over, small first-day milestones, "full in 3 h" estimates).

**Since Phase 2:** the system starts empty and is charted by survey, and stations are built round bodies the player picks rather than restored where they stood; the page moved to the Line style and the game's UI kit, and the map to a star-centred schematic with orbits (save version 2; version 1 saves start again).
