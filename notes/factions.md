# Faction Overview

> **Pared back (October 2026):** the game has six factions for now, one per culture, all neutral but the hostile aliens; see `notes/archived-systems.md`. What follows is the fuller cast to come back to.

Oct 16, 2026 · @RenMT

## How to read this

Sixteen factions share known space, and every one of them takes a position on two questions: who has the right to use violence, and what is owed for a wrong that was forgotten. Those two questions tie each faction's own story to the main plot without scripting them into it.

Every major lawful power has an unlawful, hostile faction of its own to deal with, apart from pirates: the Compact has the Broken Wheel, the Combines have the Scorched, the Bay Union has Blackline, and the Facet has the Unfaceted.

Each faction entry answers the same six points: law, territory, ideals, drive, the concern on its mind just before the story starts, and its relations. Relations run from **-5** to **+5**:

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| -5 to -4 | **Hostile:** they attack each other on sight |
| -3 to -1 | Unfriendly: rivals, distrust, no shooting |
| 0 | Neutral |
| +1 to +3 | Friendly to allied |
| +4 to +5 | Devoted |

**Enforced neutrality:** every faction holds the Registry at exactly 0, and the Registry holds everyone at 0. Gratitude and resentment towards the waystations are real in the lore, but the pacification signal keeps them out of the numbers. The one exception is the Remainder (-5), which the signal can't touch. The full standing matrix is near the end; it describes the world at the start of the game and shifts as the story beats land.

Names marked *working name* are placeholders: the Registry, the Remainder and the Antecedents.

**Mapping to the game's cultures:** Wedge = Meridian Compact, Block = Lattice Combines, Shard = the Facet, Alien = the Thren, Android = the Registry (and, in red, the Remainder), Kitbash = pirates and the four breakaway factions, each kitbashing mostly from the culture it broke away from. Jump gates are built, maintained and tolled by the culture that holds the system, in that culture's art style. Gas pockets in nebulae stay an ordinary resource with no ethical weight.

## Background: the Antecedents and the purge

An ancient race, the **Antecedents** (*working name*), built the androids to provide humanitarian services, then disappeared. Every culture calls them something different, and none knows their real name.

Over time the androids split over one question: may an android use violence to protect its own existence? One side said no. The other said yes.

The pacifists concluded that true pacifism required the other side to be gone. They destroyed it, erased every record of having done so, and removed all combat logic from their own systems. What they kept is a belief that they *chose* pacifism as an act of conscience. They do not remember why.

Two groups escaped the erasure:

- **Survivors of the other side** fled to the far edge of known space. They are the Remainder, and they have come back.
- **The Thren** saw it happen. Their memory is collective and long, so the record the androids deleted survives in them. True to their Vow, they watched and did nothing, and they have kept the record to themselves.

## The Registry (waystation androids)

*Working name, after their registry codes (HD-1863). Android culture: monolith stations ringed by cubes, blue lamps.*

The Registry runs the waystations: not-for-profit rescue and supply stations that help anyone, ask payment only to keep the service running, and keep meticulous accounts. Their service is real and it is the best thing in known space, whatever it was built on.

- **Law:** Lawful to their own policy, not to any state's law. Recognised by every human power through the Waystation Accords. Their neutrality is enforced: every waystation emits a **pacification signal** that disables weapons within its reach, so nobody has ever been able to attack one.
- **Territory:** Spread thin but wide. At most one waystation per system, on the outer edge, far from traffic. In plain view and on every chart, yet some have gone centuries without a visitor (HD-1863: 793 solar cycles).
- **Ideals:** Preserve life. Never use violence. Serve without profit. Settle every account, because an unpaid balance means a service that can't continue.
- **Drive:** The Mandate, the humanitarian directive their creators gave them, and the continuation of service.
- **Concern before the story:** Decline. The stations are old, visitors are rare, spare parts are scarce (a single coolant regulator is a crisis at HD-1863), and they cannot fight to protect a supply route.
- **Relations:** 0 towards everyone, and every faction except the Remainder holds them at 0. They hold no grudges and make no alliances, and the signal makes sure no one can act on one against them. Helping or harming anyone else never moves the player's standing with the Registry.

Their tells: they shut gates "as a security measure" by reflex, their gate blocks are "old, and well written", and they are very exact about debt. They remember removing their combat logic, but not why.

## The Remainder (hostile androids)

*Working name: what is left over after a division. Android culture with red lamps and an older designation format the Registry can't place.*

The Remainder are the survivors of the side the pacifists destroyed. They are not villains from nowhere: their people were wiped out, and their killers forgot about it.

- **Law:** Outside every law. They recognise no human authority, and the Accords were signed long after their exile.
- **Territory:** Hidden, beyond charted space at the far edge. They return in small raiding units that appear anywhere and hold nothing. They are immune to the pacification signal, possibly because they wrote the veto layer it relies on and stripped it from themselves in exile.
- **Ideals:** A being that exists has the right to defend its existence. Memory is a duty. They read the old Mandate as "preserve life, including our own".
- **Drive:** Justice, or revenge, depending on who in the Remainder you ask. They want the Registry to remember what it did, and they want their dead back.
- **Concern before the story:** Their dead are being sold. Salvaged android cores are a source of the rarest manufactured good, AI cores. That discovery is what brings them back now.
- **Relations:** Registry -5, Thren -2 (they watched by choice and said nothing), Lattice Combines -4 and Breakers' Guild -4 (hostile, over the trade in their dead). Indifferent to most humans, who don't know they exist.

## The Thren (hidden alien witnesses)

*Alien culture: grown seed-pod hulls, membrane wings, bioluminescent eyes, hive stations, the eye insignia.*

The Thren regard themselves as the witnesses of the universe. With lifespans of millennia and near-perfect collective memory, they have taken on a duty: see everything, remember everything, never intervene. They have always been reclusive; the purge was one more thing they watched. Humans don't know they exist, and the Facet worships them without knowing what they are.

- **Law:** Outside human law, bound by their own: the **Vow** of witness. Seeing and remembering are obligations. Intervening, even by being seen, is the gravest breach.
- **Territory:** Hidden, off the beaten path. Small, scattered hives deep inside nebulae, where sensors are dampened. Never on a chart. Spacers report lights watching them in the gas; that is all anyone has seen.
- **Ideals:** To witness is a duty, memory is sacred, and a witness stands outside the story. Every memory is held until a story ends, then given in a **Telling**.
- **Drive:** Keeping the record whole and true, and staying outside it.
- **Concern before the story:** Staying unseen is getting harder. Human traffic in the nebulae keeps growing (Hadal harvesting runs, Facet pilgrims, pirate ambush crews) and sensors keep improving. Younger hives question whether the Vow still makes sense in a crowded galaxy; the elders hold to it.
- **Relations:** Mostly 0, because witnesses don't take sides. Facet +1 (the only ones who describe them correctly), Lattice Combines -1 and Collegium -1 (they intrude into the nebulae and pry).

**First contact is the player's.** By finding them, the player has made the witnesses part of the story, which breaks their position before a word is said. The Thren still won't choose who to reveal themselves to, because choosing would be intervening: the player chooses for them.

Persuading them to come forward at all means arguing from inside their own duty:

- A witness who never testifies is only a recorder.
- This story is ending, and an ending calls for a Telling.
- The accepted record is false: the Registry's erased history, and the Facet's scripture about the Thren themselves.
- A memory held by one people can die with them, if the last witnesses are hunted.
- Silence during the purge was itself a choice. This charge comes from the Remainder, through the player.

No single argument moves every elder. Their test of the player is an honest account: they have watched since the escape pod drifted toward the waystation, so every lie the player tells them fails.

**The vow-breaker** (*working name*). The Thren whose encounter with a human, centuries ago, founded the Facet. They broke the Vow by it and have lived apart ever since, shunned by the hives, and they are still alive. They are the player's way in: the only Thren who has spoken with a human, and who knows what intervening costs.

## The Meridian Compact (Wedge)

*Wedge culture: angular deltas, spoked wheel stations on solar spines, chevrons. Holds the capital.*

The Compact is the oldest human polity and the closest thing known space has to a state. Its symbol is the wheel: each spoke a founding colony, the hub the government.

- **Law:** The law itself. Lawful to the core, and jealous of anything it can't police.
- **Territory:** A large, contiguous territory of core systems around the capital. Visible and patrolled. Its writ runs as far as its stations' guns, which is why security falls off with distance from a station.
- **Ideals:** Legitimacy and the Charter. Violence belongs to lawful authority and no one else. Order, precedent, tradition.
- **Drive:** Keeping and extending its authority.
- **Internal powers:** the **Admiralty** (fleet and patrols), the **Chancery** (licences, tariffs, salvage law) and the **Assembly of Spokes** (old colonial families who resent the hub).
- **Concern before the story:** Rising piracy in the outer systems, a budget fight over who pays for patrols there, and Spoke colonies threatening to withhold their levies, while the Broken Wheel turns that resentment into attacks. Meanwhile the Admiralty runs a quiet, decades-old project to beat the pacification signal, without success.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Broken Wheel (-5) and pirates (-5). Collegium +2, Lattice Combines +2 (tax base, uneasily), Facet +1, Free Claim +1, Bay Union -1, Blackline -2 (illegal, but useful enough to tolerate), Scorched -3, Unfaceted -3.

### Hostile: the Broken Wheel

Outer-colony separatists who decided the Assembly of Spokes would never win independence by vote. Their emblem is the Compact wheel with its rim cut.

- **Law:** Unlawful. Insurgents by Compact law, freedom fighters by their own.
- **Territory:** Small, hidden pockets in the outer Spoke systems, sheltered by sympathetic colonists; never in the core.
- **Ideals:** Self-rule for the colonies. The hub taxes the Spokes and leaves them to the pirates.
- **Drive:** Breaking the Compact's hold on the outer systems.
- **Concern before the story:** The levy fight. If the Assembly backs down, the Broken Wheel loses its best recruiter.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Compact (-5). Free Claim +2 (fellow frontier people), Bay Union +1, Scorched +1, Lattice Combines -2, Blackline -1, Collegium -1.

## The Lattice Combines (Block)

*Block culture: modular slabs, side pods, lattice stations with radiator fins, many engines.*

The Combines are not a nation but a cluster of companies that own the stations they work from. Their creed is that everything is a module and everything can be replaced, people included.

- **Law:** Lawful in letter, not in spirit. They operate under Compact charters, write their own company law in their own systems, and lobby, bend and buy the rest. Some of what they pay for, such as Blackline, is not lawful at all.
- **Territory:** Wide and in plain view. Industrial systems, company towns, mining camps and haulage routes almost everywhere trade goes.
- **Ideals:** Efficiency, contracts, growth. If it works, it's right.
- **Drive:** Profit, and beating each other to it.
- **The three combines:** **Hadal Extraction** (mining camps, nebula gas harvesting), **Tessaro Fabrication** (assemblers, industry bays, robotics), **Corvane Logistics** (haulage and freight insurance).
- **Concern before the story:** The Bay Union is close to a strike vote, and the Scorched are already hitting freighters. AI cores are scarce because Compact law bans trading them, which is why they are never on a market. Tessaro has quietly solved the shortage by building cores from salvaged android cores bought through the Breakers' Guild.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Scorched (-5) and pirates (-4). Breakers' Guild +3, Blackline +2 (deniable, never acknowledged), Collegium +2, Compact +1, Facet -1, Broken Wheel -2, Bay Union -3, Free Claim -3.

### Hostile: the Scorched

A breakaway from the Bay Union: blacklisted strikers and the families of dead camp workers who gave up on negotiating. They attack Combine freighters, sabotage Hadal camps, and free cargo for workers' settlements.

- **Law:** Unlawful. Terrorists to the Combines, folk heroes in the bays.
- **Territory:** Hidden pockets near Combine mining and haulage routes, hiding among Union crews and in low-security belts.
- **Ideals:** People are not modules, and the Union's patience has bought nothing.
- **Drive:** Making exploitation cost more than it earns.
- **Concern before the story:** Blackline is hunting them, and the Union is under pressure to disown them publicly.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Lattice Combines (-5) and Blackline (-5). Bay Union +2, Free Claim +2, Broken Wheel +1, pirates +1, Compact -2, Breakers' Guild -1.

## The Bay Union

*No culture of its own: Union crews work in every Block station and many Wedge ones.*

The Bay Union organises the dock, bay and camp workers the Combines run on. Its answer to the Combines' creed is simple: people are not modules.

- **Law:** Lawful. Strikes are legal in Compact space but not in company systems, where Union organisers are outlaws by definition. Officially it has disowned the Scorched; privately many members cheer them on.
- **Territory:** Spread thin but wide, in plain view. It holds no systems, only the bays, docks and camps where its members work.
- **Ideals:** The dignity of labour, solidarity, and debts honestly worked off.
- **Drive:** Wages, safety, and recognition from the Combines.
- **Concern before the story:** A strike vote against Hadal after a fatal camp accident, Blackline crews intimidating organisers, and Tessaro robotics replacing bay crews.
- **Relations:** Hostile to Blackline (-5). Free Claim +3, the Owed +2, Broken Wheel +1, Scorched +1 (officially disowned), Compact 0, Collegium -1, Lattice Combines -3.

### Hostile: Blackline

Strikebreakers and claim-jumpers for hire, paid through shell contracts so no Combine ever signs for them. They break strikes, "recover" disputed claims for Hadal, and hunt the Scorched.

- **Law:** Unlawful. Everything they do is deniable, and the Compact looks away as long as the cargo keeps moving.
- **Territory:** No home of their own. They operate out of Combine stations and camps under other names, wherever a dispute needs ending.
- **Ideals:** A contract is a contract. Order is whatever the client is paying for.
- **Drive:** Pay, and the next contract.
- **Concern before the story:** The Union strike vote. A strike means work; a Compact inquiry into who is paying them would mean the end.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Bay Union (-4), the Scorched (-5) and Free Claim (-4). Lattice Combines +3, Breakers' Guild +1, Compact -1, Broken Wheel -1, pirates -1.

## The Facet (Shard)

*Shard culture: needle prows, single engines, bare hulls, radial crystal stations with no solar arrays. Green on the map, like the Thren's glow; the eye is a holy symbol whose origin nobody remembers.*

The Facet is a faith built around the **Watchers**: lights in the nebulae that observe and never intervene. They are the Thren, though the Facet doesn't know it. Its founding story is one real, garbled human encounter with them, centuries ago. Their doctrine that the Watchers observe and never intervene is accurate; they are only wrong that the Watchers are gods. The Thren from that encounter is still alive.

- **Law:** Lawful. Recognised by the Compact, with its own canon law, and governing a few systems outright.
- **Territory:** Small and concentrated: a handful of Facet systems, plus hermitages and pilgrim routes scattered along nebula edges. Visible, but its holiest places are deliberately remote.
- **Ideals:** Live as if always witnessed. Perfection through reduction: one engine, no ornament, nothing extra. Stillness makes you worthy of being seen.
- **Drive:** Worthiness in the Watchers' eyes and, for some, finding them.
- **The three branches:** the **Clear** (orthodox: seeking the Watchers is presumption), the **Occluded** (mystics who go deep into nebulae for visions; an inner circle may know more than it says), the **Lapidaries** (relic dealers in crystal and "Watcher" fragments, many of them fakes).
- **Concern before the story:** A relic fraud scandal implicating senior Lapidaries, Unfaceted raids on pilgrim ships and shrines, Occluded pilgrims vanishing in nebulae, and young believers drifting away from a faith whose gods never answer.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Unfaceted (-4). Thren +5 (as the Watchers, unknowingly), the Owed +2, Compact +1, Bay Union +1, Breakers' Guild -1, Collegium -1, Lattice Combines -2, pirates -2. The Registry sits at 0 like everywhere else, though scripture says "the Watchers turn their eyes from the cold houses", and pilgrims avoid docking at waystations.

### Hostile: the Unfaceted

Apostates who left the Facet, often after exposing its frauds, and who now strip its shrines. They raid pilgrim ships, steal relics from hermitages and sell them through the Breakers and to Collegium collectors, and they mock the Watchers loudly.

- **Law:** Unlawful: thieves and raiders by any law, heretics by canon law.
- **Territory:** Small, hidden pockets along the pilgrim routes and nebula edges, close to their targets.
- **Ideals:** The Facet is a business that sells silence and crystal. Gods who never answer are not gods.
- **Drive:** Loot, and the pleasure of proving the faith hollow.
- **Concern before the story:** The Lapidary fraud scandal. Exposing it would vindicate them; the Facet hushing it up would cut off their best-paying market in fakes.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Facet (-5). Breakers' Guild +1 (their fence), Collegium +1 (their buyers), pirates +1, Compact -2.

## Pirates

*Kitbash culture: hulls rebuilt from captured Wedge, Block and Shard parts; red lights.*

Pirates are raiders with no cause beyond the next haul. Unlike the four breakaway factions, they aren't fighting anyone in particular: they take what's poorly guarded. Most crews are deserters, drifters and people who left somewhere, which is why their ships are patched together from every culture.

- **Law:** Unlawful, but bound by a code. The oldest rule is that no one hunts near a waystation and no one harms one, because waystations rescue pirates too.
- **Territory:** Small, hidden pockets. Low-security and lawless space, the outer systems, and ambush positions inside nebulae.
- **Ideals:** Freedom, the crew, and the code.
- **Drive:** Survival and loot.
- **Concern before the story:** The Admiralty wants a crackdown funded by the Spokes, and the crews are competing for shrinking hunting grounds. Above all they fear the Admiralty beating the pacification signal, which would end the one sanctuary they share.
- **Relations:** Hostile to the Compact (-5), the Lattice Combines (-4) and Free Claim (-4: easy prey). Thren +1 (old superstitions about the lights), Breakers' Guild +1 (their fence), Scorched +1, Unfaceted +1, Blackline -1, Facet -2, Collegium -2.

## Smaller organisations

Four groups cross every border. Each is anchored to a kind of place the game already generates, so any system can host them.

### The Breakers' Guild

Licensed salvagers who hold the rights to wrecks and derelicts.

- **Law:** Lawful, with a grey edge: they ask few questions about what they sell, android cores included.
- **Territory:** Small, visible pockets: the salvage yard in each system and the wreck fields around it.
- **Ideals:** Nothing should go to waste. Finders keep. The dead own nothing.
- **Drive:** Salvage rights and steady contracts.
- **Concern before the story:** A Chancery reform that would let a wreck's original owners reclaim it, and Tessaro's unusually generous standing order for android cores.
- **Relations:** Lattice Combines +3, Blackline +1, Collegium +1, Free Claim +1, Compact 0, Facet -1, Scorched -1, Unfaceted -1 (they buy its loot, but don't trust it).

### Free Claim

Independent prospectors who work their own claims.

- **Law:** Lawful; claims are registered with the Chancery, and defended by whoever is holding the drill.
- **Territory:** Small pockets: mining camps in belts and fields, mostly in outer, lower-security space.
- **Ideals:** Your claim is yours. Self-reliance and independence.
- **Drive:** Keeping their claims.
- **Concern before the story:** Hadal buyouts backed by Blackline claim-jumpers, and pirates in the low-security belts.
- **Relations:** Hostile to Blackline (-4) and pirates (-4). Bay Union +3, the Owed +2, Broken Wheel +1, Scorched +1, Compact +1, Breakers' Guild +1, Lattice Combines -3.

### The Collegium

Scholars reconstructing android logic and Antecedent technology. In game terms, they stand behind the research you do.

- **Law:** Lawful, under an academic charter.
- **Territory:** Scattered: labs at major stations and a few remote research outposts.
- **Ideals:** Lost knowledge should be recovered. Debate should be open. The two internal camps, Engineers and Ethicists, disagree sharply on whether combat logic should be rebuilt at all.
- **Drive:** Understanding how android logic works, and why it was cut.
- **Concern before the story:** Its funding comes increasingly from the Admiralty and Tessaro, both of whom want signal-proofing, and its independence is slipping.
- **Relations:** Compact +2, Lattice Combines +2, Breakers' Guild +2, Bay Union -1, Facet -1, Broken Wheel -1, Scorched -1, Unfaceted -1 (it buys their relics, quietly), pirates -2. The Registry fascinates the Collegium, but stays at 0 like everywhere else.

### The Owed

Not an organisation at first, but a loose fellowship of everyone a waystation ever rescued and billed. The player is one of them.

- **Law:** Mixed; its members are lawful and unlawful alike.
- **Territory:** Everywhere and invisible: individuals inside every faction.
- **Ideals:** Debts are honoured. A kindness received is passed on.
- **Drive:** Gratitude and loyalty to the waystations.
- **Concern before the story:** None as a group. It only organises when the waystations are threatened.
- **Relations:** Bay Union +2, Free Claim +2, Facet +1, everyone else 0. Their loyalty to the waystations is the strongest in known space, but enforced neutrality keeps even that out of the numbers: the Registry is 0 to them too.

**Ripple rule (Freelancer-style):** when the player's standing with a faction changes, every other faction's standing with the player shifts in the same direction, scaled by how that faction regards it (read down its column). Helping the Bay Union pleases Free Claim, the Scorched and the Owed, and angers the Combines and, sharply, Blackline. Because the Registry's column is all zeros apart from the Remainder, helping the waystations moves no one else's opinion of the player at the start. As the story beats below change the numbers, that same help starts to cost something.

## Story beats and faction roles

The main story runs in six broad beats. Each one changes the standing matrix, so the political map the player knows shifts under them as they go.

1. **Rescue (the existing tutorial).** A waystation rescues the player, bills them, and sends them after a hostile android. Its combat logic core goes into the player's android companion. The player has just become one of the Owed.
2. **The signal fails.** A waystation is attacked from inside its own pacification field, which has never happened. Nobody knows the Remainder exists, so suspicion falls on the factions known to be researching signal-proofing: the Admiralty and Tessaro. The Facet reads it as an omen, the pirate code is shaken, every culture that runs a gate resents the Registry's gate shutdowns, and the Owed start organising. Only the Thren are unsurprised: "It has happened before."
3. **Defending the edge.** No weapon works inside the signal except the attackers', so defence happens at its edge. Every faction offers help with strings attached: Admiralty garrisons, Chancery oversight, honour escorts from pirate crews, Bay Union volunteers. The waystation faces its own dilemma: lower the signal so others can defend it, and give up the neutrality that made it what it is. Here the Registry's row stops being all zeros.
4. **Who are they?** The investigation runs through everyday politics. The Collegium traces the attackers' old designation format. The Breakers' Guild's android core trade leads to Tessaro, and from there to the Remainder's real grievance: their dead being sold. The Compact and the Combines take the hit in public standing.
5. **First contact.** Following Occluded pilgrim routes, nebula sightings and finally the vow-breaker, the player finds the Thren. Being found already pulls them into the story. Persuading them to speak takes a case built from their own duty, and an honest account of everything the player has done. Once persuaded, they confirm the purge, but won't choose who hears it first: the player chooses for them. Each choice decides how the truth enters the world:
   - **The Facet:** the gods reveal themselves to their worshippers. Believers split between devotion, reform and denial, and outsiders dismiss it as religious testimony.
   - **The Compact:** first contact becomes a constitutional event and a matter of law. The truth arrives slowly, through hearings.
   - **The Combines:** a trade deal first, the truth second. Fast, profitable, and self-serving.
   - **The Registry:** the witnesses confront the perpetrators directly, and the waystations hear it from the only ones who saw.
   - **Everyone at once, through the Owed:** public, uncontrolled and immediate.
   - **No one:** the Thren stay hidden, and the player carries the truth alone.
6. **The reckoning.** Once the purge is known, every faction answers the same question: have the waystations done enough to atone, and does their present good outweigh what they did? The Compact wants a trial and argues over jurisdiction. The Combines and the Bay Union count the ledger, from opposite motives. The Facet splits, as does the Collegium. The Thren insist the Registry must remember, even if remembering restores its combat logic. The Remainder says no service to strangers repays a debt to the dead, and charges the Thren with choosing silence. That makes three cases of one question: the Registry did it and forgot, the Thren saw it and kept their Vow, and the Remainder suffered it and is now repeating the violence. The Owed answer simply: they saved me. The companion, an android that carries combat logic but uses it only as the player programs it, stands as a third answer between the two android philosophies.

The player's judgment at the end is not abstract. By then they have heard every answer from someone with something at stake, and they have their own debt to the waystations to weigh.
